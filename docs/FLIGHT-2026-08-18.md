## 1. `departure` counts as "has flown", so holding short derives as LANDED

**ROOT CAUSE. It explains 2 and 3 outright.** Under discussion; see the
conversation this file was written in.

    derive("departure", on_ground=True) -> "landed"      # never left the ground

`phases.ON_THE_GROUND` is `clearance, taxi, holding_short, landed, taxi_in` and
does not contain `departure`, so `has_flown("departure")` is True. You are IN
`departure` from the moment Tower has you, and most of that is spent stationary
on the runway.

    15:20:12  PILOT  Kobuleti Tower, sockeye, holding short, runway 7,
                     ready for departure
    15:20:12  .. phase: departure -> landed

## 2. Departure hands an outbound aeroplane back to Tower, to thirteen miles

**Consequence of 1.** A landed aeroplane is Tower's, so the ladder kept
returning him. Twelve times in the log:

    .. Sockeye: Kobuleti Departure -> Kobuleti Tower, landed,  5 nm, outbound
    .. Sockeye: Kobuleti Departure -> Kobuleti Tower, landed, 13 nm, outbound

The pilot: *"the departure controller told me to check in with tower, as though
I'm on the ground."*

This is #164's shape — *an airborne aeroplane is never Ground's* — one rung up,
and its guard could not fire: he was not airborne in the engine's opinion. Worth
asking whether that guard should read the sim rather than the phase, which is
the same question 1 asks.

## 3. Tower answers a read-back with "go ahead"

**Consequence of 1**, and the engine had it right:

    15:20:30  PILOT  Clear for takeoff, runway seven, sockeye.
    .. refused an unauthorised handoff: Sockeye, that's correct, contact
       Kobuleti Departure one two three decimal three airborne, good day.
    15:20:36  ATC    Sockeye, Kobuleti Tower, go ahead.

The engine composed the correct transmission and a guard discarded it, so the
agent fell back to a greeting. Tower→Departure was judged unauthorised for an
aeroplane the board thought had landed.

The pilot: *"I just gave Kobuleti Tower a read back, and he said, go ahead
afterwards. Don't know what that's all about."* Note this is the SAME SYMPTOM as
the open #10 C3 (*"field in sight" answered with "go ahead"*) from a different
cause — a read-back or a report being answered as though it opened the
conversation. Two causes, one thing a pilot hears.

## 4. "Advise you have information Whiskey" — asked five times after he said it

**FIXED, 18 August.** Independent of 1. Off the engine's own decisions, not the
agent's replies — five, not three:

    15:12:48  PILOT  Kobuleti Clearance, sockeye, with whiskey.
    15:12:51  ATC    ... Advise you have information Whiskey. Say your request.
    15:13:11  ATC    ... Advise you have information Whiskey. Say your request.
    15:13:39  PILOT  ...I do have information whiskey
    15:13:43  ATC    ... Advise you have information Whiskey. Say your request.
    15:14:47  ATC    ... Advise you have information Whiskey. Say your request.
    15:15:43  ATC    ... Advise you have information Whiskey. Say your request.

**This entry originally said the opposite and was wrong.** It read *"the letter
DOES reach the aeroplane — `ac.atis_letter` is set"*, and diagnosed an emitter
not consulting a record that already existed. Written from reading the code,
which showed a schema field, a column, a view and a `hydrate` — everything
except a value. `ac.atis_letter` was empty on every call of every sortie ever
flown, and the controller was structurally incapable of stopping.

Two faults, stacked, either of which alone would have caused it:

    bedrock_intent.classify   asks the model for `atis_letter` in
                              INTENT_SCHEMA and never reads it off the
                              response. `Intent.atis_letter` was always ""
    intents.dispatch          wrote it only under `case IntentKind.CHECK_IN`,
                              so it would have been dropped on the request
                              and read-back calls even once populated

The two writers of `ac.atis_letter` are `dispatch` and `hydrate` — and
`hydrate` restores the column the board flushed *from that same field*. A
closed loop with no source.

Why nothing caught it: the field existed, typed, defaulting to `""`. Every
reader compiled, every test constructing an `Intent` by hand passed, and an
unfilled letter is indistinguishable from a pilot who never said one. It needed
a second transmission to show, and only a live pilot sends one.

Fixed in both places, plus a schema-coverage check so a field we ask the model
for cannot be silently discarded again — `tests/test_he_only_has_to_say_the_letter_once.py`.

The pilot: *"That should probably be something in the database to record that I
have whiskey, so he doesn't keep asking."* The column was right; nothing wrote it.

## 5. A named plan still gets the two-plan disambiguation

**FIXED, 18 August — #182**, and the rebuild it argues for is #183.

    15:13:08  PILOT  Roger Sock, I would like Batumi Test, IFR to Batumi.
    15:13:20  ATC    two plans fit that -- say which: transit and recovery
                     filed as Batumi Test, or transit and recovery filed as
                     Domino.

He named it. #1 G3/G4 owns "which plan a spoken request means, and when to ASK
instead of picking one" -- the asking is right and firing it on an unambiguous
request is not.

**I guessed this was a consequence of 4 and it was not.** The reasoning was
that `request_clearance` short-circuits on the ATIS letter, so the engine never
ran plan resolution and the model improvised the question -- which fitted,
because the disambiguation is not among the engine's decisions for the sortie.
Running the resolver directly on a clean board settled it:

    'Batumi Test'   ->  AMBIGUOUS ['BatumiTest', 'Domino']

The bare name, nothing else in the transmission, against two plans. The engine
genuinely could not tell them apart, and no amount of reading the transcript
would have shown that.

`score` gives 100 points for naming a plan outright and the test was
`label in said` -- a substring of the transcript. `batumitest` is not inside
"i would like batumi test", and never will be, because a label is TYPED and a
request is SPOKEN. Only `destination` scored, worth one point and shared, so
they tied.

**The filing rule is what made it certain.** `_LABEL_OK` requires one word with
no spaces (migration 012: *"Samovar One"* and *"Samovar Two"* are how the wrong
sortie gets cleared), so anybody wanting a two-word name types `BatumiTest` --
a spelling no pilot will ever say.

    "i remember that weeks ago, the flightplan ux said that a FlightPlan label
     must be one word. BatumiTest must not be one word. This 'splains why"

And the rule enforces its letter rather than its reason: `SamovarOne` passes it
today, so the confusable pair is still filable. That, plus the hand-weighted
point system the fix lives inside, is #183.

## 6. `/diag` shows "cleared for" blank after a clearance was issued

    15:13:52  ATC    Sockeye, Kobuleti Clearance, cleared to Batumi, as filed,
                     maintain five thousand, ...
    15:18:16  NOTE   it says I asked for BatumiTest, which I asked for, that is
                     correct, but cleared for showing blank. Did I not get
                     cleared for BatumiTest? What's the status?

**Look at this one next, and not only as a display fault.** `assign_approach`
reads the same cleared-plan path (`_cleared_plan_now(known)["approach"]`), and
it is the ONLY thing that gives an aeroplane `ac.profile`. If the value is
genuinely absent rather than merely unrendered, then this sortie had no
procedure assigned at any point -- and #177's asking flow, which only runs when
`_pro(ac)` is None, would have been reachable for the wrong reason while the
approach half was never armed at all.
